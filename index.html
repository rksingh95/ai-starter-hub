Start here: 6 things, in this order

About 15 minutes a day. Use made-up or anonymised material until you trust your process.

1

Learn to ask

Give a role, the context, the task and the format you want. “Summarize this” gets weaker answers than “From these notes, list decisions and next steps in a table.”

2

Learn to check

AI can sound sure and still be wrong. Spot-check names, numbers and dates against your source. Ask it what it is unsure about.

3

Pick 3 everyday tasks

Summarise, draft, organise. Choose things you already do weekly and compare the AI version with your own.

4

Use your own material

Notes, documents, emails, with names and private details removed. Answers based on your own text are easier to verify.

5

Make it a routine

Save the prompt that worked, add a short checklist, reuse it. A repeatable workflow beats a clever one-off.

6

Then try agents

An agent is AI that takes several steps with tools for you. Start small, keep a human approval step and review what it actually did.

Meeting notes to tasks Tested 
Turn these meeting notes into a table:
Task | Owner | Deadline | Missing information

Use only the facts provided. Write "Not specified" for missing owners or deadlines. Do not invent details. Distinguish completed items from open actions. Finish with the questions we need to resolve.

Notes:
[paste your notes with names and private details removed]

Check: Compare every row with your notes, look for invented details, turn “Thursday” into a real date yourself. I ran this once in ChatGPT (Instant mode, 31 Oct 2026) on fictional notes; one test is not proof it always works. One-page checklist (PDF).

Ask me questions first Template 
I want help with: [task].
Before you answer, ask me up to three questions you need to do this well. Wait for my answers, then give your response.

Check: Were the questions actually useful? If not, say what is missing and rerun.

Facts, inferences and unknowns Template 
Read the text below. List every important statement under three headings: Fact (stated in the text), Inference (your reasoning from the text), Unknown (not in the text). Do not add outside information.

Text:
[paste text]

Check: Look for anything under “Fact” that is not literally in your text.

Explain it simply, and tell me what you are unsure about Template 
Explain [topic] to someone with no background. Use short sentences, define any technical term, and give one everyday example. At the end, list what you are unsure about and what I should verify elsewhere.

Check: Verify any number, date or claim you plan to rely on with a primary source.

Compare options Template 
Compare [option A] and [option B] for [my situation]. Use a table with these criteria: [criteria]. Mark any cell where you are not sure. Then list what I should verify before deciding.

Check: Prices, versions and features change; confirm on the official pages.

Draft in my voice Template 
Here is a sample of how I write:
[paste 1–2 short paragraphs you wrote]

Write a [email/message] about [topic] in the same tone. Keep it under [number] words. Do not add facts I did not give you.

Check: Read it aloud. Remove anything that sounds like you would not say it, and anything you did not tell it.

Say no politely and clearly Template 
Someone asked me to [request]. I cannot do it because [reason]. Write a short, polite reply that says no clearly, does not over-apologise, and offers [alternative, or "no alternative"].

Check: Does it say what you mean, and nothing you did not mean?

Be my editor, do not rewrite Template 
Act as a careful editor. Review the draft below and list specific problems: unclear sentences, missing information, tone issues. Quote the sentence for each problem and explain why. Do not rewrite the draft.

Draft:
[paste draft]

Check: Decide yourself which suggestions to accept.

Summarise a long text into 3 points Template 
Summarise the text below in three bullet points. Keep all numbers and dates exactly as written. Add one line: "What this does not say."

Text:
[paste text]

Check: Compare each number and date with the original.

Decision brief Template 
I must decide: [decision]. Facts I know: [facts]. Write a one-page brief: the options, the main risks of each, what we do not know yet, and what information would change the decision. Do not invent facts.

Check: Any fact in the brief that I did not provide is an error to remove.

Checklists you can reuse

Check any AI answer in 3 passes

  • Accuracy: names, numbers, dates against the source.
  • Completeness: what is missing, who is not mentioned.
  • Usability: could someone act on this tomorrow?

Before you paste anything

  • No passwords, keys or tokens.
  • No client or customer data, no health or financial data.
  • No names or details of colleagues unless your policy allows it.
  • Check your company’s AI policy first. If unsure, ask.

AI at work, carefully (Germany & Europe)

Not legal advice. Rules and tools differ by employer and change over time. This page is general information from a software engineer, not a lawyer.

  • Policy first. Read your employer’s AI policy and use approved tools. If there is none, ask before pasting work material into a public AI tool.
  • Data. Do not paste personal data, client data, secrets or confidential code unless your organisation has approved that tool and use.
  • Removing names is not anonymising. Context (roles, projects, dates) can still identify people. Treat “names removed” as lower risk, not safe.
  • You stay responsible. Check AI output before you rely on it or send it on.

Primary sources to read (check for newer versions):

  • European Commission: AI Act. Official page for the EU AI Act, including how its rules phase in.
  • BSI: Generative AI Models, opportunities and risks (German Federal Office for Information Security, version dated 21 Jan 2025).
  • EDPB Opinion 28/2024 on personal data and AI models (17 Dec 2024).

AI in plain words

Prompt
What you type to the AI: your question or instructions.
Hallucination
When AI states something wrong or made-up in a confident way. It can happen with any current tool, so you check.
Context
The information you give the AI for a task. More relevant context usually means a more useful answer.
Agent
AI that takes several steps using tools (for example search, files, email) toward a goal, instead of one reply. Keep a human approval step.
Anonymised vs. names removed
Anonymised means a person can no longer be identified by any reasonable means. Removing names alone often does not reach that.
Source
Where a fact comes from. A good answer lets you trace each fact to one.
